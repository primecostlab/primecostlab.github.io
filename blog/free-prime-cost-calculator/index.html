Free Prime Cost Calculator for Restaurants, Bars and Cafes: What to Enter and How to Read It

Published 2026-10-08 · 4 min read

The short answer

Prime cost is your cost of goods sold plus your total labor, divided by your sales. For full-service restaurants, Restaurant365 (n.d., accessed 2026-10) gives 60% to 65%. For bars, Backbar Academy (2019) says "ideally" 55% to 65%. For cafes, Prima Coffee Equipment (2021) gives a ceiling of about 60%.

A free calculator should do three things. It should use your real inventory. It should show food, beverage and labor separately. It should compare each one with a source you can check.

The formula in words

Cost of goods sold = beginning inventory + purchases − ending inventory.

Prime cost = cost of goods sold + total labor.

Prime cost % = prime cost ÷ total sales.

Each ratio can also be done on its own:

  • Food cost % = food cost of goods sold ÷ food sales.
  • Beverage cost % = beverage cost of goods sold ÷ beverage sales.
  • Labor cost % = total labor ÷ total sales.

What to enter

You need nine numbers for one month:

  1. Food sales.
  2. Beverage sales.
  3. Beginning inventory, food.
  4. Food purchases.
  5. Ending inventory, food.
  6. Beginning inventory, beverage.
  7. Beverage purchases.
  8. Ending inventory, beverage.
  9. Total labor, including employer payroll taxes and benefits.

Total sales is food sales plus beverage sales.

The two inventory counts are the hard part. Count at close, on the same day each month, and write the date next to the number. The ending count of one month is the beginning count of the next, so you only count once per month.

Worked example (fictional bar)

This is a made-up food-serving neighborhood bar. Food and drinks each bring in half of the sales. The numbers are invented to show the math. They are not data from a real business.

| Line | Food | Beverage | |---|---|---| | Sales | $50,000 | $50,000 | | Beginning inventory | $4,000 | $9,000 | | Purchases | $15,200 | $10,400 | | Ending inventory | $4,200 | $9,400 | | Cost of goods sold | $15,000 | $10,000 | | Cost % | 30% | 20% |

Total sales are $100,000. Labor for the month, with taxes and benefits, is $35,000, which is 35% of sales.

Prime cost is $15,000 + $10,000 + $35,000 = $60,000. That is 60% of $100,000.

Now compare each ratio with its source:

  • Food cost 30%: inside 28% to 35%, MarginEdge (2026). This is a general restaurant figure. No bar-specific food cost source was found.
  • Beverage cost 20%: inside 18% to 24%, BevSpot (2016). BevSpot says the median bar sits just above 20%. The data is old, so treat it as a guide.
  • Labor cost 35%: at the top of 25% to 35%, Sculpture Hospitality (2025). This is a vendor blog, so it is a secondary source.
  • Prime cost 60%: inside 55% to 65%, Backbar Academy (2019). That range is not bar-specific.

All four are in range. Labor sits at the very top of its range, so it is the one to watch. Each point of labor on $100,000 of sales is $1,000 a month.

What to do when prime cost is high

When prime cost goes above the range, one of the two halves is the cause. These are the usual reasons.

  1. Inventory counts are skipped or rushed. Cost of goods sold is wrong, and so is every ratio after it.
  2. Free pours and over-portioning. In a bar, a heavy pour or an unrecorded comp raises beverage cost without raising sales.
  3. A schedule built for the busiest night. Labor stays high on the nights that do not need it.
  4. Price increases not passed on. Your supplier's price went up. Your menu price did not.
  5. Waste or theft not tracked. If cost rises and nothing else changed, loss is possible. Count more often to find where it starts.

What I do this week

  1. Choose the closing day of the month and count inventory that night. Do food and beverage separately.
  2. Collect the month's purchases from your invoices.
  3. Total labor from payroll, including employer taxes and benefits.
  4. Calculate the three ratios and prime cost with the formulas above, or in the free calculator.
  5. Write each result beside its source range. Mark the one that is furthest out.
  6. Pick one action on that ratio only. Do not change everything at once.

FAQ

What is a free prime cost calculator? It is a tool that takes your cost of goods sold and labor for a month and divides them by your sales. A good one also shows food, beverage and labor separately, and compares each one with a source.

Is prime cost the same as food cost? No. Food cost is only the cost of the food you sold, as a percentage of food sales. Prime cost adds beverage cost and total labor, so it covers your two biggest controllable costs.

How often should I calculate prime cost? Monthly at minimum, using inventory counts at both ends of the month. If you can count more often, you will find problems sooner.
